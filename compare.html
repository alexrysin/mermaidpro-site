What actually matters when choosing

1. Where does your diagram code live?

Some apps store your source on the vendor's servers; some in page attachments; the best store it in the page itself. If the vendor disappears (it happens — the Connect-era app freeze of 2025-2026 orphaned several), you want your source in your pages. Mermaid Diagrams Pro stores source as macro data inside the page: it's in your exports, your page history, your copies.

2. Does it follow Confluence's dark mode?

The single most common complaint in this category's reviews: white diagram boxes on dark pages. Test it: switch your Confluence theme and watch whether the diagram re-renders. (Ours does, instantly, without a refresh.)

3. Can readers actually read large diagrams?

Zoom and fullscreen sound trivial until you publish a 40-node flowchart. The official Mermaid Chart app's most-repeated review complaint is diagrams rendering tiny with no way to zoom.

4. Is the app actively maintained?

Check the listing's version history. The legacy Stratus app — long the category leader — hasn't shipped a release since September 2025, and its recent reviews report critical errors with no vendor response. Mermaid evolves fast; a frozen app falls behind the language itself.

5. Where does rendering happen?

Apps with the Runs on Atlassian badge render entirely inside Atlassian's infrastructure — no external servers see your diagram code. If your security team reviews vendors, this badge shortens that conversation considerably.

Our honest take on each

Atlassian Labs viewer — free and fine for an occasional diagram in a code block. It's an unsupported Labs experiment: rendering is slow on big pages, auto-detection misses code blocks, and there's nobody to email when it breaks.

Mermaid Chart (official) — made by the company behind mermaid.js, and best if your team already lives in their SaaS editor. As a Confluence experience it disappoints: reviews consistently cite small fixed-size renders, missing zoom, and broken dark mode.

Stratus Mermaid Diagrams — was the category standard for years and stores source in attachments (good!). But it's been frozen for a year, sits on the deprecated Connect platform, and unresolved bugs are piling up in reviews. If you're on it, plan your exit — see our migration guide.
